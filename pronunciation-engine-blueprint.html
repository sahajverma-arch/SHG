04

The AI voice module, decided

Revised — no-card constraint

Azure's F0 tier asks for a card at signup for identity verification only — it won't auto-charge unless the resource is manually upgraded off F0. If a card anywhere is still a hard no, the field narrows fast once you also filter for Hindi support.

OptionCard to sign upHindi support
Azure AI Speech — Pronunciation Assessment Card — verify only, F0 never auto-bills Yes — hi-IN confirmed
SpeechAce (dedicated pronunciation API) No card No — en, fr, es only
AssemblyAI No card, $50 credit No — not in their language list
Deepgram No card, $200 credit Yes
Self-hosted Whisper (Hindi-tuned) No card, no account at all Yes — best Hindi accuracy of the no-card options

SpeechAce and AssemblyAI are out immediately — SpeechAce is purpose-built for exactly this (phoneme-level pronunciation scoring) but has no Hindi at all, and AssemblyAI doesn't list Hindi either. That leaves Deepgram and self-hosted Whisper as the only no-card options that work for Hindi — and neither returns a pronunciation/fluency score out of the box the way Azure and SpeechAce do. Both are transcription engines; the accuracy score has to be built on top, by comparing the recognized text against the expected passage.

Between the two no-card finalists

OptionPronunciation accuracyEffort
Deepgram (hosted API) Word-level transcript + confidence only — score built from edit-distance vs. the expected passage Medium — no model hosting, but scoring logic still custom
Self-hosted vasista22/whisper-hindi-large-v2 Most accurate Hindi transcript of the no-card options — fine-tuned on Hindi corpora, not generic multilingual Whisper — same custom scoring approach as Deepgram, but a better transcript to score against High — you host the model yourself
Recommendation

Self-hosted vasista22/whisper-hindi-large-v2, no account or card required. It's the most Hindi-accurate free option among everything here that doesn't need a card, since it's fine-tuned specifically on Hindi speech rather than generic multilingual Whisper. Pair it with a custom scorer for v1: align the recognized text to the expected passage and score by edit-distance plus per-word confidence — a reasonable proxy, not true phoneme-level scoring.

Deepgram is the easier fallback if you'd rather not manage model hosting — same no-card, Hindi-capable profile, $200 free credit, hosted API — at the cost of the same scoring gap.

If this prototype turns into something you'll actually ship, Azure's Pronunciation Assessment is still the only genuinely plug-and-play option that returns real phoneme/fluency/completeness scores for Hindi — worth revisiting then, since the card costs nothing as long as the resource stays on F0.
